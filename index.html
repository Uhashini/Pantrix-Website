Addressing Blind Spots in Everyday Decisions

Who Needs PANTRIX?

Current budgeting and fitness apps completely isolate food data from financial data. PANTRIX connects what you spend with what you consume across evolving lifestyle priorities:

Budget-Conscious

“I know how much I spent, but not where my money is going.”

The Pain Point: Unnoticed spending patterns and invisible budget leaks on low-value, ultra-processed items.

  • EWMA expenditure baseline & tolerance tracking
  • Automatic detection of price creep and impulse markups
  • Itemized line-item unit rate breakdown
"PANTRIX reveals whether 30% of your budget is leaking into low-value packaged snacks."

Health-Conscious

“I want to eat better, but I don't know what my purchases add up to.”

The Pain Point: Nutrition info exists on the box, but not at the overall basket or purchasing level.

  • UN FAO NOVA ultra-processed food classification
  • Aggregate food-group diversity (unprocessed vs ultra-processed)
  • Transparent dietary quality scores from single receipt scan
"Connects what you buy at the supermarket with your long-term metabolic health."

Fitness-Focused

“I want to hit my protein goals without spending unnecessarily.”

The Pain Point: Hard to manually calculate nutritional yield against actual grocery rupees spent.

  • Protein per ₹100 cost-efficiency calculation
  • Essential Amino Acid (BCAA/EAA) quality breakdown
  • Weekly hypertrophy protein target attainment tracking
"Identifies highest-yield protein sources so you don't overspend on fitness fuel."

One User, Evolving Needs

“My lifestyle priorities shift constantly.”

The Solution: Users focus on budget one month and fitness the next. PANTRIX adapts without forcing app switching.

  • Seamless Tri-Mode toggle (Money • Health • Muscle)
  • Single unified database (PostgreSQL + pg_trgm)
  • Zero duplicate receipt entry or multiple isolated apps
"A unified system connecting financial decisions with dietary consumption."

Stage 01 Deep Dive

Preprocessing & Perspective De-warping

OpenCV • Python
Why is this stage necessary?

Mobile cameras capture receipts under uneven shadows, creased angles, and thermal printer fading. Running OCR directly on raw photos degrades recognition accuracy below 60%. Preprocessing flattens and normalizes lighting to maximize character edge contrast.

Technical Mechanism & Algorithm

1. Convert BGR image to LAB color space, apply CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) on L-channel.
2. Detect receipt perimeter corners via cv2.Canny() and cv2.approxPolyDP().
3. Compute 3x3 homography matrix with cv2.getPerspectiveTransform() and warp to clean top-down rectangle.

Input Raw JPEG/PNG Mobile Photo (1080p-4K, BGR NumPy Array)
Output Flattened, Contrast-Equalized Rectangular Matrix (np.ndarray)
clahe_dewarp.py 
# Stage 1: Lighting Normalization + Dewarping
def preprocess_receipt(image_bytes):
    img = cv2.imdecode(np.frombuffer(image_bytes, np.uint8), cv2.IMREAD_COLOR)
    # CLAHE on L-channel in LAB space
    lab = cv2.cvtColor(img, cv2.COLOR_BGR2LAB)
    l, a, b = cv2.split(lab)
    clahe = cv2.createCLAHE(clipLimit=2.0, tileGridSize=(8,8))
    cl = clahe.apply(l)
    normalized = cv2.cvtColor(cv2.merge((cl,a,b)), cv2.COLOR_LAB2BGR)
    
    # 4-Corner Homography Perspective Correction
    corners = detect_quadrilateral_corners(normalized)
    M = cv2.getPerspectiveTransform(corners, target_rect)
    dewarped = cv2.warpPerspective(normalized, M, (W, H))
    return dewarped
